# proof360 — why

*What it's for. Not how it works (`README.md`), not the full history (`docs/CHANGELOG-SARVESH.md`).*

## Why it exists

A founder gets judged on trust long before anyone reads their code — by investors doing
diligence, by enterprise buyers sending security questionnaires, by insurers pricing
cover. The evidence they need is scattered, and nobody tells the founder what's missing
until it costs them the deal.

proof360 reads a company from what's publicly there, shows the gaps, and routes each gap
to something that closes it. No login to see your own report — if you have to sign up
before you learn anything, it's a lead form wearing a diagnosis.

The diagnosis is the front door. The business is the routing.

## What changed lately

**26 Aug — nothing on screen is invented any more.** Three surfaces were showing made-up
data as though measured: a hardcoded "18,420 tokens processed", and a settings panel
telling whoever was signed in that they had $10k of AWS credits under review and a Vanta
subscription renewing in June. All fiction, shown to real people about their own accounts.
*Inventing a number is bad. Inventing an entitlement states a commercial relationship that
doesn't exist, to the person it supposedly belongs to.*

**26 Aug — the record got a surface.** We'd been accumulating claims about a founder for
months and showing them a single number: "6 things we've noted so far." They could see we
knew six things and had no way to see what, let alone correct one.
*A product that says it holds your evidence has to show it to you.*

## How we knew

433 tests across 64 files, all passing, none skipped — run today, not quoted from a log.
Every AWS and Microsoft program on screen now traces to the trigger that earned it
("matched because your stage is Seed and your infrastructure is aws"), and the demo
fixtures can no longer leak into a real company's view.

## What's next

**Ask before you grade.** Onboarding still opens by presenting a report about someone who
hasn't told us anything. Turning that into questions first is the difference between
guiding a founder and grading them.

**Show CORPUS's citations.** The gap API already returns evidence with its source; the
screen drops it. We sell "show the work" and then don't.

**Make the score explainable.** A number nobody can account for is a grade, and we don't
grade.

*None are scheduled. Dates go here when John sets them, not before.*

## What we're deliberately not doing

**Not putting the report behind a login.** The moment you must register to see your own
gaps, we're an ad.

**Not grading.** Hold a lamp so the founder sees the next steps they could choose from.
Scores that rank people make them defensive, and a defensive founder tells you nothing.

**Not inventing numbers — live or illustrative.** If there's no measurement behind a row,
the row shouldn't exist.
